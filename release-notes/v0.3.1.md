# v0.3.1: A proper DirectInput effects engine, and car-matched rev lights in Assetto Corsa

Games send their force feedback as a set of different effects: a constant
force, plus springs, dampers, friction, inertia and rumble. We only took the
constant force value, so everything else went quiet the moment we took the
wheel. Now TF4ALL plays them all. Car-matched rev lights, new in v0.3.0, reach
Assetto Corsa too, taking their switch-on revs and their colours from the car's
own files.

### The game's DirectInput effects now play

- **What was missing.** Trueforce wheels render a game's spring, damper, friction, inertia and rumble in their own firmware, and all of it stops the moment the plugin opens a Trueforce session. Since v0.1.12 that has left every game running on raw force alone, which is why the same game could feel lighter and less detailed with the plugin on than with it off.
- **What changed.** The plugin now reads them off the USB connection as the game sends them and renders them into the Trueforce stream alongside the game's force, so you get what the wheel's firmware would have produced plus Trueforce on top. Assetto Corsa's stationary damper is back, for one.

### Automatic LED pattern matching comes to Assetto Corsa

- **Your wheel lights up like the car you are driving.** Cars in Assetto Corsa can carry their own shift light data: the revs each one comes on at, the colours they use, and how fast they flash at the redline. The plugin now reads that from the car itself and puts it on your rim, so the lights fill and change colour at the same revs as the dash in front of you, and flash along with it. Choose "Auto (this car's own colors)" once and every car after that brings its own. Cars that model none keep the pattern you picked, and a pattern you have pinned to a car still wins.
- **You will need the TF4ALL CSP Bridge**, from Game Mods. Assetto Corsa drives the wheel's lights itself, so the plugin asks it to step back, and puts that setting back if you turn our rev lights off.

### Bug fixes

- **The wheel no longer pulls to one side when you pause.** When a game is paused, SimHub does not stop sending telemetry. It keeps repeating the same readings it had at the moment you paused, so the plugin went on treating them as live and held the force from that instant, pulling the wheel all the way over. Some games say outright that they are paused, and the plugin now listens for that. For the ones that do not, it recognises the repeated readings itself. Either way the wheel settles until the game is running again. First reported in RaceRoom.
- **Your rev lights no longer stick, go dark, or turn up late.** v0.3.0's new bridge let the wheel's screen work in Assetto Corsa for the first time. That turned out to have a consequence nobody saw coming: with the screen switched on, it and the game were both talking to the wheel at once, and the game's lights lost. They could stick where they were, go dark, then jump to the right level a few seconds later. Only one thing writes to them at a time now, and the plugin works out which by watching the wheel itself, so the lights go to whoever they belong to. Changing pattern mid-session stops flickering for the same reason.
- **No more unplugging your wheel to get force feedback working.** For some users the plugin's FFB capture would not work at all until they unplugged the wheel and plugged it back in. The plugin now detects a wheel that is not responding and tries to recover it on its own, with no replug needed.

### Interface

- **The pattern preview is shorter.** It used to fill the strip, empty it again, and only then settle on the finished pattern. Now it fills once and holds, which shows the direction the lights run in and the colours themselves without the wait.
- **The What's new window is wider, resizable, and easier to read.** Wrapped notes render as paragraphs instead of running together.
