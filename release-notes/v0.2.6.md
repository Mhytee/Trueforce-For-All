# v0.2.6: A Drive tab for the phone dash

The TF4ALL dash shipped in 0.2.5 as a way to change settings from your phone.
This release gives it something to show you while you are actually driving.

### The Drive tab

A screen of information boxes you arrange yourself. Gear, speed and revs sit in
the middle, with pedals and steering around them, and the rest of the card is
yours: pick what goes in each box from the dash itself or from Settings. Keep
one layout for everything, or a different one per game.

Boxes available now: tyre temperatures, a friction circle, a g meter, fuel, lap
times, a radar, the relative, driver inputs, damage, the FFB trace, race flags
and more.

Which boxes a game can fill depends on the telemetry it publishes, so not every
box is offered everywhere. Turning on "forward telemetry to SimHub" is
recommended when using the dash, though we read what we can from the game
directly to keep it from being necessary.

Some of what is in there:

- **Tyre temperatures** shows the tyre temperatures and shifts colour from blue
  to deep red.
- **The friction circle** shows how much grip is left.
- **Lap times** shows your lap times and the delta.
- **Race flags** across the top.

### Idle mode

Park up or close the game and the dash turns into an ambient card: your name
and number over moving artwork, with the plugin version and any waiting update
along the foot. Tap the dash or start driving to leave idle.

Ten backgrounds to choose from. Set how long the dash waits before it drops
into idle, or turn it off entirely.

### Themes

Eight palettes, picked in Settings and applied to the running dash without a
reload. A theme repaints the box outlines, buttons, tiles and idle artwork.
Text stays white or grey in every one, and colours that mean something, like a
red warning or a green best lap, are never repainted.

### The wheel's own screen (experimental)

The G PRO and RS50 have a small display in the middle of the wheel that normally
shows the wheel's menu. It can now show what you are doing instead, right where
you are already looking.

Five ready-made screens: a big gear with the speed beside it, a big speed with
the gear beside it, speed above the gear, and two that carry the lap delta. The
screen goes back to the wheel when you stop driving.

Or build your own. Pick one of the wheel's layouts, then choose what goes in each
of its slots, the same way you arrange the boxes on the Drive tab. Fields
available: gear, speed, speed with its unit, lap delta, position, lap of total,
and last lap time. Any slot can also hold your own text, so you can label a value
or put a word on the screen that means something to you. The wheel decides how
big each slot is drawn and where it sits, so the editor tells you the size and
the character limit of every slot before you fill it.

Lap delta is only offered where a game actually reports one, using the same
evidence the Drive tab uses to grey out boxes a game cannot fill. In a game with
no lap timing, the delta stays empty and the panel says why.

The screen reacts as you drive. Shift and it flashes the gear, unless the screen
you chose already shows it. Cross the line and it gives you the lap: the time on
its own for a personal best, the time over the delta for anything else.

It speaks up on its own, too. It names a preset when one loads, shows the new
number when a bound button changes a gain, tells you the strength Auto strength
settled on for the car you are in, and warns you there when the game is still
sending its own force feedback. A wheel that is fighting the game outranks
everything else, and a shift never buries something rarer.

Off by default, and it needs Telemetry Based FFB for the same reason the rev
lights do: writing to the screen while a game runs its own force feedback makes
that force feedback cut out, because they share one channel on the wheel.

How the screen is driven was worked out by PeposCJ for the LogiDynamicDash
project, on an RS50, and documented in mescon's Linux driver. Thanks to both.
Tested here on a G PRO, which answers the same way. This is new ground, so tell
us how it goes.

### Also

- **Changing Trueforce gain from a wheel button now shows the new value on the
  dash.** Bind it in SimHub's Controls tab.
- **The dash readout also names a preset when one loads, and reports the strength
  Auto strength settled on for the car you are in.**

### Bug fixes

- **The G923 PS honours the game's stop command.** It is the one supported
  wheel that speaks the older Logitech protocol rather than HID++, where a
  force plays until the game cancels it. That cancel was being ignored, so a
  force could keep running after the game had dropped it. This is the most
  likely cause behind the occasional sharp pull to one side on that wheel.

- **Forza telemetry is read correctly.** Forza reports tyre temperatures in
  Fahrenheit and numbers its gears differently from its own documentation, and
  both were taken at face value. Temperatures now appear in the unit you picked
  in SimHub, and the gear matches the one the game shows.
