This update has been a long time coming. I have been working on it in the background ever since the plugin first launched, and it is by far the biggest release yet.

&nbsp;

Stability has come a long way, and that has been a community effort the whole way. Every shared log and every detailed issue report has uncovered something new about how these wheels actually talk to the PC, and "my wheel does not work" reports have dropped sharply as a result. There is more still to figure out together, and the picture of what is happening under the hood is clearer than it has ever been.

### Community features

The biggest win in this release: **once one person figures out a car's redline, fixes its name, or picks its engine layout, everyone driving the same car gets that information automatically**. The plugin's community half is now live.

- **Community preset browser** built into the Presets tab. Browse what other drivers have shared for any game or car, sorted by votes and downloads. Hover for a description; click to switch in.
- **Share your presets and packs.** Game presets, car presets, custom engines, and multi-preset packs all go through the same Share flow. Pick which games a preset targets, add a description, hit Share.
- **Car facts contribute back automatically.** When you correct a redline, name a car, or pick its engine layout, the plugin offers to send that fact to the community. The next driver loading that same car gets your correction applied without ever opening the panel.
- **Rate the presets you have downloaded.** Up and down votes from anyone who has downloaded the preset, surfaced as the sort order for the community browser.
- **Stay up to date with downloaded presets.** When a curator publishes an update to a preset you have downloaded, an "updates available" chip surfaces it. Apply the update manually, or have unedited downloads pick up curator updates automatically (optional setting).
- **Account controls and stats.** Sign in with a one-time code emailed to you (no password to manage). Change your sharing display name, see how your shared presets have been received, export your data, or delete your account.

Your username is not shown on submitted data. No single bad submission can overwrite the consensus: presets are surfaced by votes, and car facts converge as more drivers submit agreeing values.

### Car facts

For each car you drive, the plugin tracks facts about it: engine layout, redline RPM, a display name (especially helpful when the game only gives you an ID like "Car_2267"), and any custom engine you have set.

Getting these right matters for how the wheel feels. The Engine pulse effect models the actual firing pattern of each cylinder, so accurate cylinder count and layout are what make a V8 feel different from an inline 6 on the wheel. The rev limiter buzz needs the real redline RPM to fire at the right moment, and some games (Forza Horizon 4, 5, and 6 among them) do not report a redline in their telemetry at all. Auto-detection handles most cases well, but when it is wrong a single community correction now propagates to every driver who loads that car next.

- **Variants are detected silently from telemetry.** When telemetry says "this engine is different from any I've seen for this car," the plugin creates a row in the background. No prompt, no modal. Works for Forza in-game engine swaps (where mid-session cylinder and max RPM change) and on every game where telemetry exposes enough signals.

### UI/UX

The whole plugin moved to a tab-based layout. The old long-scroll panel is gone; everything sits behind Effects, Presets, Controls, and Settings tabs that mirror how other SimHub plugins are organized.

- **Tab-based layout for the whole plugin.** Effects, Presets, Controls, and Settings each get their own tab. The Advanced settings pop-up is gone; FFB tweaks are grouped under a single collapsible section.
- **Presets tab.** Sortable, searchable, filterable view of every preset you own and every pack you've installed. Three segments (Games / Cars / Custom engines) plus an Installed packs view, with a persistent toggle remembering what you last looked at. Game chips filter the car list and combine with search for fine-grained narrowing. Multi-select to mass set defaults, share a pack, or batch delete.
- **Type precise values on every effect slider.** Every slider's number can be clicked and typed directly for exact tuning, not just dragged.
- **SimHub home-screen Trueforce gain widget.** The Trueforce master gain and audio capture gain live in a widget inside SimHub's home Feedback section, next to the Motors and Wind tiles, so you can adjust them without opening the plugin panel.

### Master gain control mapping

Two new actions appear in SimHub's Controls and events list: **Master gain up** and **Master gain down**. Bind them to any button or input and adjust the Trueforce gain on the fly without opening the plugin panel.

- **Bind any button or input.** Both actions show up in SimHub's Controls and events list; bind them like any other SimHub action.
- **Adjustable step.** Each press changes master gain by the step value in Settings (default 0.05), clamped at 0 and 2.

### Rev limiter

The rev limiter no longer asks you to think in percentages of max RPM.

- **Engagement is set as an absolute RPM** instead of "% of max RPM." Existing presets that were tuned with a custom threshold are migrated to the equivalent absolute value on first use, on a per-car basis.
- **Community-shared redlines apply automatically.** When the community has confirmed a real redline for the car and engine variant you are driving, the rev limiter uses it instead of estimating. So a stock AE86 and an AE86 with a 4AGE swap each get their own community redline, picked up automatically when telemetry says you have switched to the other one. Especially valuable for games like Forza that don't expose redline in their telemetry.

### Forza Horizon

- **Every current FH6 car ships with a human-readable name.** The plugin now carries a baked-in name table for all of Forza Horizon 6's cars, so they show up as "1997 Mazda RX-7" instead of "Car_2267" out of the box.

### Bug fixes

- **Forza Horizon UDP misconfiguration no longer goes limp.** If FH was configured to send UDP telemetry to SimHub instead of directly to the plugin (Data Out pointing at SimHub's port instead of the plugin's), the plugin would take over the wheel anyway and the wheel's FFB would go limp. The plugin now detects that no Forza packets are arriving, falls back to SimHub telemetry instead of taking over, and surfaces a persistent setup banner pointing you to the right port so you can get the enhanced telemetry path working without losing FFB.

### Upgrading from older versions

When you update, your existing presets are backed up first and then migrated to the new system automatically; you should not have to touch anything. The backup lives alongside your normal settings file in SimHub's `PluginsData/Common/` folder, with a timestamped `.bak-user-preset-migration-...` filename so it stays out of the way. Keep it or delete it once you have confirmed everything still feels right.

If you had a rev-limiter engagement percentage saved on a preset, it converts to a real redline RPM value the next time you drive the car it applies to, on a per-car basis.

### Latency and responsiveness

This release sharpens the wheel's response even further: a more consistent feel when your system is under load, and lower latency for audio capture haptics.

- **Keeps feedback steady when your PC is under load.** The force-output and audio-capture threads now run at Windows' multimedia real-time priority (MMCSS "Pro Audio"), the class the system audio engine uses for itself, so a busy CPU is far less likely to interrupt them into a hitch or click.
- **Haptics generated from captured game audio reach the wheel sooner.** The capture helper now delivers audio in smaller chunks, typically cutting the audio buffer from about 8 ms to 4 ms, with no added risk of dropouts.
