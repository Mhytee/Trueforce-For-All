# v0.2.7: iRacing support, rev-light fixes and per-car rev-light patterns

Full iRacing support is here: the sim's own steering force carried by TF4ALL,
with the effects, the wheel's screen and the rev lights all running at once.
Users can now set the wheel's lighting pattern from TF4ALL, with the option
to save a pattern per car.

### iRacing: the sim's own force, TF4ALL's effects

iRacing keeps publishing the steering torque it wants the wheel to hold even
with its own force feedback switched off. TF4ALL reads that torque straight
from the sim's shared memory and carries it to the wheel at 1 kHz, so the
force you feel is iRacing's own, with TF4ALL's effects layered on top of it.

- **iRacing's feel, TF4ALL's effects.** In other games, Telemetry Based FFB
  builds the force from telemetry; here there is nothing to build. The force
  is the sim's own, the effect stack rides on top, and a strength slider
  scales the base against the sim's own maximum.
- **The wheel's screen and rev lights work in iRacing.** Normally they share
  a channel with the wheel's force; with TF4ALL carrying the force, that
  channel is free. iRacing also states its force-feedback switch outright,
  so it needs no USB capture for the lights and screen.
- **Incident points, announced.** The dash shows your count against the
  session's limit. Each new point announces itself for a moment, and the
  wheel's screen flashes what it cost ("2x") as it happens.
- **Engines sound like themselves.** iRacing states each car's cylinder
  count, and a shipped table adds the crank layout, so a cross-plane American
  V8 pulses like one instead of a generic even-fire. Your own engine pick and
  the community's data still win over the table.
- **Setup is two switches**, and a first-launch notice walks you through
  them: turn iRacing's own force feedback off (off, not strength zero), and
  set loadTrueForceAPI=0 in app.ini.

### Set the wheel's rev-light pattern from the app, and per car

A G PRO or RS50 stores lighting patterns for its rev strip: four built-in
sweeps and five custom slots you may have designed in G HUB. Until now the
only way to choose between them was the wheelbase's own menu, or per game in
G HUB. Now they are in the plugin, and for the first time they can follow the
car. The G923's strip has one fixed look, so it gets no picker; its fix is
further down.

- **Pick a pattern, see it immediately.** A picker in Settings (and on the Car
  facts panel) lists every pattern by direction. Picking one sets the
  wheelbase's selection, exactly as if you had used the wheel's own menu, and
  plays one rev sweep on the strip so you see what you chose.
- **A car can remember its own.** One click on "Remember for this car" and the
  pattern comes back whenever that car loads. Drive something with a
  distinctive real-world light layout and your wheel can match it; forget it
  again with one click and nothing else changes.
- **Colors and direction stay yours.** The wheel renders its own stored
  patterns; the plugin only selects which one is active. Custom slots keep
  whatever you built in G HUB.

Picks apply immediately, in and out of game. Changing the pattern while a
game is sending its own force feedback briefly interrupts that force, so it
is best done parked.

### Rev-light fixes

After this update, rev lights are meant to simply work in iRacing, Forza and
Farming Simulator. If yours stay dark or fill wrong, report it on GitHub or
the Discord so it gets fixed.

- **G923 rev lights either never lit, or filled wrong.** On the Xbox G923
  the lights never worked at all. And while a G923 has the same ten LEDs as
  a G PRO or RS50, its fixed layout lights them two at a time, five steps
  instead of ten, so a ten-step fill read as a full bar from half revs.
  Both are fixed: the Xbox G923 now speaks the same protocol as the
  G PRO, and the plugin now asks the wheel its strip length instead of
  assuming ten. I only own a
  G PRO, so this is not yet confirmed on real G923 hardware. If you have
  one, PLEASE let us know how it goes on GitHub or the Discord, working or
  not.
- **Your chosen pattern survives.** Starting the lights used to switch the
  wheel's stored pattern selection to the outside-in sweep every session.
  Now the wheel is asked first and an already-chosen pattern is left alone.
- **Dark lights explain themselves.** A wheel that refuses light commands now
  says so in the log instead of looking like success while nothing lights.

Protocol credit where it is due: the strip-length and refusal findings come
from mescon's Logitech Trueforce Linux driver work, and the pattern staging
and commit sequence builds on captures by PeposCJ and our own.
