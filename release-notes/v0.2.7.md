# v0.2.7: Full Assetto Corsa and iRacing support, the LIGHTSYNC tab, and guides in the app

Two games get full support in one release: iRacing and Assetto Corsa. The
sim's own force feedback, TF4ALL's effects, the wheel's screen and the rev
lights all run at once. The wheel's lights get a tab of their own, with a
pattern maker, a library bigger than the wheel's five slots, and colors that
can automatically match the car you are driving. Guides and setup
instructions now live in a dedicated help browser.

### iRacing: the sim's own force, TF4ALL's effects

iRacing keeps publishing the steering torque it wants the wheel to hold even
with its own force feedback switched off. TF4ALL reads that torque straight
from the sim and carries it to the wheel at 1 kHz, so the
force you feel is iRacing's own, with TF4ALL's effects layered on top of it.

- **iRacing's feel, TF4ALL's effects.** In other games, Telemetry Based FFB
  builds the force from telemetry; here there is nothing to build. The force
  is the sim's own, the effect stack rides on top, and a strength slider
  scales the base against the sim's own maximum.
- **Each car can have its own Max force.** Cars follow the max-force number
  iRacing already keeps for them, so existing tuning is respected. The plugin
  also watches what a car really pushes as you drive, the same idea as
  iRacing's own auto button: drive a clean lap, and once the reading holds
  steady, one press takes it. Nudge any car heavier or lighter from there
  without touching Strength.
- **Traction loss reads the sim's slip angle.** The effect now takes the
  game's slip angle into account for a more accurate effect, and the
  iRacing preset ships the settings tuned on it.
- **The wheel's screen and rev lights work in iRacing.** Normally using
  them interrupts the wheel's force; with TF4ALL carrying the force,
  nothing is interrupted.
- **Incident points, announced.** The dash shows your count against the
  session's limit. Each new point announces itself for a moment, and the
  wheel's screen flashes what it cost ("2x") as it happens.
- **Engines sound like themselves.** iRacing states each car's cylinder
  count, and a shipped table adds the crank layout, so a cross-plane American
  V8 pulses like one instead of a generic even-fire. Your own engine pick and
  the community's data still win over the table.
- **Two switches in iRacing, two in the plugin.** A first-launch notice walks
  you through all four: set loadTrueForceAPI=0 in app.ini and turn iRacing's
  own force feedback off (off, not strength zero), then set the mode to Normal
  and tick "Take over force feedback for iRacing".

### Assetto Corsa: full support through the TF4ALL CSP Bridge

Assetto Corsa can now hand the plugin its force feedback directly. A small
CSP script, the TF4ALL CSP Bridge, does the handover: the plugin offers to
install it the first time it sees the game, and the Game Mods section in
Settings installs or removes it any time after. It needs Custom Shaders
Patch and one restart of the game.

- **The wheel feels exactly as you have tuned it.** The bridge hands over
  the game's finished force, after your in-game gain and every CSP FFB
  tweak, and TF4ALL carries it to the wheel with the effects on top.
- **The wheel's screen and pattern changes stop costing force.** With the
  bridge carrying the force, the Dynamic OLED display runs in Assetto
  Corsa, and switching LIGHTSYNC patterns no longer cuts the force
  feedback.
- **No USB capture needed.** With the bridge installed the plugin gets the
  force from the game directly, so Assetto Corsa no longer depends on
  USBPcap. Without the bridge, everything keeps working through the capture
  as before. There is no setting to manage.

### The wheel's lights get a tab of their own

LIGHTSYNC sits beside the FFB tab. A G PRO or RS50 stores lighting patterns
for its rev strip, and until now the only way to choose between them was the
wheelbase's own menu or G HUB. The G923's strip has a fixed layout, so the tab
stays hidden there.

- **The wheel's light pattern can automatically match the car you're driving.**
  With "Match my wheel to the car I'm driving" ticked, the strip takes on each
  car's own colors and fill direction as you get in, lighting where the real
  car lights. The colors come from Lovely Sim Racing, shared under
  CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, and a car they have not covered keeps whatever pattern you
  chose. Coverage is sim-racing: Le Mans Ultimate and Automobilista 2 are the
  best covered today, then iRacing, Assetto Corsa Competizione, the F1 titles
  and Assetto Corsa EVO.
- **A library bigger than the wheel.** Your wheel stores five patterns; the
  library holds as many as you like, and a bound button walks the whole
  library without taking your hands off the wheel.
- **A pattern maker.** Ten LEDs, click one and color it, with the wheel
  showing the whole pattern as you work. Mirror copies the LED you have
  selected onto the one opposite, so a symmetrical bar gets built a pair at a
  time. Reverse turns the strip round for a ramp built from the wrong end.
- **Thirteen hand-made patterns built in**, yours to copy and edit.
- **Color correction, pre-tuned.** The three colors inside an LED are not
  equally bright, so an even mix of red and green can reach the rim looking
  lime instead of yellow. Three sliders under Color trim correct the balance,
  shipped already set from measurements on a G PRO.
- **A car can remember its own.** One click on "Remember for this car" and
  the pattern comes back whenever that car loads; forget it with one click.
- **The TF4ALL Dash's rev strip can match your wheel's.** On Auto, the
  default, it takes its colors, fill direction and switch-on points from
  the wheel's rev lights; pick a direction instead for the plain ramp.

Picks apply immediately, in and out of game. In a game whose own force
feedback the plugin passes through, changing the pattern interrupts that force
for a moment, so it is best done parked. Where the plugin drives the force
itself, in iRacing, in Assetto Corsa with the bridge, or with Telemetry
Based FFB, there is nothing to interrupt and you can change it on the move.

### A new mode: Lightsync only

The master switch used to be on or off. It now has a middle position,
Lightsync only, which leaves a game's own force feedback completely alone
while still setting the wheel's pattern for the car you are in. Useful for
games that support Trueforce natively. Each position is remembered per game.

Upgrading keeps your per-game choices, with one change: a game you had
switched off comes back as Lightsync only. If you want a title silent, set
it back to Off and it stays there.

### Guides, in the app

A "?" in the header opens a guides browser, so the information you need to get
set up and the questions people ask most are easy to find.

- **Setup for the games that need it, and what to do when something is wrong.**
  iRacing's two switches, Forza's Data Out and forwarding, the Farming
  Simulator mod, a wheel gone limp, and why the rev lights only work in some
  games.
- **Search reads the guides, not just their titles.** Type G HUB, app.ini or
  5300 and you land on the one that explains it.
- **Each guide opens what it is about.** Read the Forza steps, press the
  button, and the UDP settings are in front of you.
- **A guide can jump into the panel.** Links inside a guide open the tab or
  the section they talk about.

### Rev-light fixes

After this update, rev lights are meant to simply work in iRacing, Forza and
Farming Simulator. If yours stay dark or fill wrong, report it on GitHub or
the Discord so it gets fixed.

- **G923 rev lights either never lit, or filled wrong.** The Xbox G923's never
  worked at all, and a G923 lights its ten LEDs in five pairs, so a ten-step
  fill read as full from half revs. Both are fixed: the PlayStation
  G923's lights are confirmed on hardware, and the same commands light a
  G PRO running in its G923 compatibility mode, so the Xbox G923 is expected
  to follow.
- **Redlines and blink rate reach every wheel.** A car's per-gear redlines
  and its blink rate apply even on a wheel whose strip has a fixed look, so
  the shift cue and the redline flash land right on a G923 too.
- **Your chosen pattern survives.** The lights used to overwrite the pattern
  on the wheel every session; now a pattern you picked is left alone.
- **The flash fires at the car's redline, not the limiter, and starts in step.**
  The Lovely data records where the real car's dash lights up, gear by gear,
  and the flash follows it, even per gear. Every burst now begins lit.
- **The lights keep up.** The strip updates at 40 ms instead of 160, so a fill
  climbs smoothly instead of stepping.

### Fixes and polish

- **A game streaming its own Trueforce no longer makes the wheel whine.**
  Native titles default to Lightsync only, but set to Normal, or in iRacing
  before `loadTrueForceAPI` is off, the game and the plugin both streamed to
  the wheel and it whined (#29). The plugin now sees the game's stream on the
  USB capture and drops to Lightsync only for that session, with the status
  panel saying why. To run the plugin's Trueforce in such a game, the game's
  own still has to be switched off; pick Normal again afterwards. MAIRA's RPM
  lights option streams the same way, and the plugin steps aside for it too.
- **The FFB tab, revamped.** Every game gets the same tab now: FFB scale at
  the top, the force feedback tweaks that lived on the Effects tab moved in
  beside it, and the telemetry-based tuning shows only for the games that
  can use it.
- **Game mods live in one place.** The Farming Simulator mod and the TF4ALL
  CSP Bridge are cards in a Game Mods section, with versions, install and
  remove.
- **Effects survive a parked car.** Sitting parked could mute the effects
  and the stationary spring; both now keep working.
- **Experimental FFB detection is no longer experimental.** A game's force
  can arrive on a USB report the capture used to ignore unless an
  experimental checkbox was on. The capture now detects which report carries
  the force and follows it, so the checkbox is retired and the force no
  longer vanishes.
- **Cycling a pattern mid-session no longer fights the game's own rev bar.**
- **A G PRO in G923 compatibility mode is recognized.** The wheel reports
  itself as a G923 there; the plugin now tells the two apart and drives the
  lights the way that mode wants.
- **Auto force can be bound.** One press runs the current game's auto force
  calibration from the rim: iRacing sets the max force from the learned
  peak, and Forza re-levels the auto strength.
- **Resuming from a pause eases the force back in.** The wheel used to snap
  from the pause spring straight to the full live force; now it fades in
  over a moment.
- **The wheel's screen can show nothing.** Pick Nothing and the wheel's own
  built-in display shows instead, while shifts, finished laps and warnings
  still take the screen for a moment, then hand it back.
- **The wheel lets go when you alt-tab out, instead of pulling to full lock.**
  Alt-tab mid-drive without pausing and some games leave their last force
  applied, spinning the wheel to one side. TF4ALL now releases it. On by
  default, in Settings, Force feedback (advanced). Tested in Wreckfest 2.
- **The Farming Simulator mod installs, repairs and removes on demand.**
  Install, Reinstall and Remove now sit in Settings, so the mod can go in
  before you start the game, be repaired later, or be taken back out.
  Uninstalling the plugin from Windows removes the mod too.
- **A wheel that refuses light commands says so.** It used to look like
  success in the log while nothing lit.
- **A diagnostic claimed every effect was off while effects were playing.**
  It only counted seven of them, so axle slip, kerb thump, lockup judder,
  collision, the rev limiter and the implement thud went uncounted.
- **Misrouted Forza telemetry now explains itself.** If Telemetry Based FFB
  is on but the game's telemetry points at the wrong port, a banner says
  what happened, with a button that jumps to the setting that fixes it, and
  whatever force feedback the game is still sending passes through instead
  of being muted.
- **Auto strength status messages are improved.** A status line, and the
  dash, now show how much of the current car the strength learner has seen.
  The learner keeps refining on its own as you drive; the Re-learn button
  just gets it there faster when a reading went bad, like a crash spiking it.
- **The per-gear redline editor moves behind a toggle in Widgets and
  display.** Saved per-gear values keep working whether the editor is shown
  or not.
- **The UI is less cluttered, and the explaining is centralized.** The settings
  had accumulated a lot of help text, most of it repeated or wanted only once.
  Over 25% of it is gone, and what was worth keeping moved into tooltips and
  the new guides browser.

Protocol credit where it is due: everything the plugin knows about programming
a light slot, the staging and commit sequence included, comes from mescon's
Logitech Trueforce Linux driver and its protocol specification, as do the
strip-length and refusal findings. PeposCJ's first-party captures settled when
the wheel's own effect selection has to be left alone, which is why the pattern
you chose survives.
