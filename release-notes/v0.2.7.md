# v0.2.7: iRacing support, the LIGHTSYNC tab, and guides in the app

Full iRacing support is here: the sim's own steering force carried by TF4ALL,
with the effects, the wheel's screen and the rev lights all running at once.
The wheel's lights get a tab of their own, with a pattern maker, a library
bigger than the wheel's five slots, and colors that can match the car you are
driving as you get in. Guides and setup instructions now live in a dedicated
help browser.

### iRacing: the sim's own force, TF4ALL's effects

iRacing keeps publishing the steering torque it wants the wheel to hold even
with its own force feedback switched off. TF4ALL reads that torque straight
from the sim's shared memory and carries it to the wheel at 1 kHz, so the
force you feel is iRacing's own, with TF4ALL's effects layered on top of it.

- **iRacing's feel, TF4ALL's effects.** In other games, Telemetry Based FFB
  builds the force from telemetry; here there is nothing to build. The force
  is the sim's own, the effect stack rides on top, and a strength slider
  scales the base against the sim's own maximum.
- **Each car can have its own Max force.** Cars follow the max-force number
  iRacing already keeps for them, so existing tuning is respected. The plugin
  also watches what a car really pushes as you drive, the same idea as
  iRacing's own auto button: drive a clean lap, and once the reading holds
  steady, one press takes it. Nudge any car heavier or lighter from there
  without touching Strength.
- **The wheel's screen and rev lights work in iRacing.** Normally they share
  a channel with the wheel's force; with TF4ALL carrying the force, that
  channel is free.
- **Incident points, announced.** The dash shows your count against the
  session's limit. Each new point announces itself for a moment, and the
  wheel's screen flashes what it cost ("2x") as it happens.
- **Engines sound like themselves.** iRacing states each car's cylinder
  count, and a shipped table adds the crank layout, so a cross-plane American
  V8 pulses like one instead of a generic even-fire. Your own engine pick and
  the community's data still win over the table.
- **Two switches in iRacing, two in the plugin.** A first-launch notice walks
  you through all four: set loadTrueForceAPI=0 in app.ini and turn iRacing's
  own force feedback off (off, not strength zero), then set the mode to Normal
  and tick "Take over force feedback for iRacing".

### The wheel's lights get a tab of their own

LIGHTSYNC sits beside the FFB tab. A G PRO or RS50 stores lighting patterns
for its rev strip, and until now the only way to choose between them was the
wheelbase's own menu or G HUB. The G923's strip has a fixed layout, so the tab
stays hidden there.

- **The wheel's light pattern can automatically match the car you're driving.**
  With "Match my wheel to the car I'm driving" ticked, the strip takes on each
  car's own colors and fill direction as you get in, lighting where the real
  car lights. Nothing to pick, nothing to remember. It works even in games that
  light the wheel themselves. The colors come from Lovely Sim Racing, shared
  under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, and a car they have not covered keeps whatever pattern
  you chose. Coverage is sim-racing: Le Mans Ultimate and Automobilista 2 are
  the best covered today, then iRacing, Assetto Corsa Competizione, the F1
  titles and Assetto Corsa EVO. It keeps growing.
- **A library bigger than the wheel.** Your wheel stores five patterns. The
  library holds as many as you like and swaps the extras into one slot you
  lend it as you drive, so a bound button walks the whole library without
  taking your hands off the wheel.
- **A pattern maker.** Ten LEDs, click one and color it, with the wheel
  showing the whole pattern as you work. Mirror copies the LED you have
  selected onto the one opposite, so a symmetrical bar gets built a pair at a
  time. Reverse turns the strip round for a ramp built from the wrong end.
- **Thirteen shipped patterns**, curated on hardware rather than chosen from
  hex, and yours to copy and edit.
- **Color correction, pre-tuned.** The three colors inside an LED are not
  equally bright, so an even mix of red and green can reach the rim looking
  lime instead of yellow. Three sliders under Color trim correct the balance,
  shipped already set from measurements on a G PRO, where red came out the
  dimmest. Whether that holds on every wheel is something we would like to
  find out, so if yours looks off, retune it and tell us. Correction can also be
  switched off per pattern, so a single pattern that looks wrong can be left
  out of it.
- **A car can remember its own.** One click on "Remember for this car" and the
  pattern comes back whenever that car loads. Drive something with a
  distinctive real-world light layout and your wheel can match it; forget it
  again with one click and nothing else changes.
- **The phone's rev strip can be your wheel's.** The TF4ALL Dash drew its own
  green to red ramp while the wheel beside it drew the car's real one, so the
  two disagreed on the same screen. On Auto, which is the default, the strip
  takes its colors, its fill direction and its switch-on points from your
  wheel's live rev lights. Pick a direction instead and you get the ramp,
  which is also what Auto falls back to on a wheel whose lights cannot be
  read.

Picks apply immediately, in and out of game. In a game whose own force
feedback the plugin passes through, changing the pattern interrupts that force
for a moment, so it is best done parked. Where the plugin drives the force
itself, in iRacing or with Telemetry Based FFB, there is nothing to interrupt
and you can change it on the move.

### One switch, three positions

The master switch used to be on or off. It now has a middle position,
Lightsync only, which leaves a game's own force feedback completely alone
while still setting the wheel's pattern for the car you are in. That is what
the games with their own Trueforce want, and it is where iRacing starts.

Each position is remembered per game when you choose it while a game is
running, and taken as your stance everywhere when you choose it at the desk
with nothing running. So a title where you want the car's pattern but none of
our force feedback costs you nothing in the others.

Upgrading keeps your per-game choices, with one deliberate change: a game you
had switched off comes back as Lightsync only rather than off. Off used to be
the only way to say "not the force layer here", and Lightsync only says exactly
the same thing while still setting your wheel's pattern for the car you are in.
If you really do want a title silent, set it back to Off and it stays there.

### Guides, in the app

A "?" in the header opens a guides browser, so the information you need to get
set up and the questions people ask most are easy to find.

- **Setup for the games that need it, and what to do when something is wrong.**
  iRacing's two switches, Forza's Data Out and forwarding, the Farming
  Simulator mod, a wheel gone limp, and why the rev lights only work in some
  games.
- **Search reads the guides, not just their titles.** Type G HUB, app.ini or
  5300 and you land on the one that explains it.
- **Each guide opens what it is about.** Read the Forza steps, press the
  button, and the UDP settings are in front of you.

### Rev-light fixes

After this update, rev lights are meant to simply work in iRacing, Forza and
Farming Simulator. If yours stay dark or fill wrong, report it on GitHub or
the Discord so it gets fixed.

- **G923 rev lights either never lit, or filled wrong.** The Xbox G923's never
  worked at all, and a G923 lights its ten LEDs in five pairs, so a ten-step
  fill read as full from half revs. Both are fixed. The protocol is confirmed
  on G923 hardware by the Linux driver project but not yet on ours, so tell us
  how it goes.
- **Your chosen pattern survives.** Starting the lights used to overwrite
  whatever pattern you had chosen on the wheel, every session. Now the wheel is
  asked first, and a pattern you already picked is left alone.
- **The flash fires at the car's redline, not the limiter, and starts in step.**
  Telemetry reports where the engine limits, which is not where you shift. The
  Lovely Sim Racing data records where the real car's dash lights up, gear by
  gear, and that is the number the flash now uses, so it can even move with the
  gear: the M4 GT3 redlines at 7150 in first and 7250 in sixth. The flash also
  ran off a free clock, leaving the wheel dark while the car was lit as often
  as not; every burst now begins lit.
- **The lights keep up.** The strip updates at 40 ms instead of 160, so a fill
  climbs smoothly instead of stepping.

### Fixes and polish

- **The wheel lets go when you alt-tab out, instead of pulling to full lock.**
  Alt-tab mid-drive without pausing and some games leave their last force
  applied, spinning the wheel to one side. TF4ALL now releases it. On by
  default, in Settings, Force feedback (advanced). Tested in Wreckfest 2.
- **The Farming Simulator mod installs, repairs and removes on demand.**
  Install, Reinstall and Remove now sit in Settings, so the mod can go in
  before you start the game, be repaired later, or be taken back out. The
  button used to exist only inside a banner shown while the game was running
  without it. Uninstalling the plugin from Windows removes the mod too.
- **A wheel that refuses light commands says so.** It used to look like
  success in the log while nothing lit.
- **A diagnostic claimed every effect was off while effects were playing.**
  It only counted seven of them, so axle slip, kerb thump, lockup judder,
  collision, the rev limiter and the implement thud went uncounted.
- **Misrouted Forza telemetry now explains itself.** If Telemetry Based FFB
  is on but the game's telemetry points at the wrong port, a banner says
  what happened, with a button that jumps to the setting that fixes it, and
  whatever force feedback the game is still sending passes through instead
  of being muted.
- **Auto strength status messages are improved.** A status line, and the
  dash, now show how much of the current car the strength learner has seen.
  The learner keeps refining on its own as you drive; the Re-learn button
  just gets it there faster when a reading went bad, like a crash spiking it.
- **The per-gear redline editor moves behind a toggle in Widgets and
  display.** Saved per-gear values keep working whether the editor is shown
  or not.
- **The UI is less cluttered, and the explaining is centralized.** The settings
  had accumulated a lot of help text, most of it repeated or wanted only once.
  Over 25% of it is gone, and what was worth keeping moved into tooltips and
  the new guides browser.

Protocol credit where it is due: everything the plugin knows about programming
a light slot, the staging and commit sequence included, comes from mescon's
Logitech Trueforce Linux driver and its protocol specification, as do the
strip-length and refusal findings. PeposCJ's first-party captures settled when
the wheel's own effect selection has to be left alone, which is why the pattern
you chose survives.
