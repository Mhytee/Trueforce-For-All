# v0.4.0: A DirectInput effects engine, RaceRoom support, and LED pattern matching in Assetto Corsa

The biggest gap in our force feedback accuracy is now fixed. This release
brings a DirectInput effects engine to TF4ALL, so the springs, dampers,
friction and waveforms a game sends reach the wheel instead of being dropped
on the floor. RaceRoom hands its force feedback over the way iRacing and
Assetto Corsa already do, which frees your rev lights and the wheel's screen
to work while you drive. Our lighting features now work in Assetto Corsa,
including automatic LED pattern matching, taken from the car's own data.

### A DirectInput effects engine

The largest single gap in how accurately we reproduce a game's force feedback,
and it affected every game we capture over USB rather than any one title.
Until now we sent the wheel a fraction of what the game actually asked for.

- **What was missing.** Games send their force feedback as a set of different effects: a constant force, plus springs, dampers, friction, inertia and waveforms. The wheel renders them in its own firmware. Until now, our FFB capture only took the constant force value, leaving force feedback incomplete. That is why the same game could feel lighter and less detailed with the plugin on than with it off.
- **What changed.** The plugin now reads them off the USB connection as the game sends them and renders them into the Trueforce stream alongside the game's force.
- **Assetto Corsa's damper is back.** On both routes: through the capture, and through the CSP Bridge, where the game's damper is now carried across and rendered into the stream. A released wheel no longer oscillates and full lock no longer arrives hard.
- **A pause menu centers the wheel the way the game intends.** While the game is paused or in the background, its spring and damper keep playing and only its steering force is silenced, so the wheel settles instead of going limp or holding a pull.

### RaceRoom hands its force feedback over

RaceRoom joins iRacing and Assetto Corsa. Turn it on with "Take over force
feedback for RaceRoom" on the FFB tab.

- **RaceRoom publishes the simulator's FFB torque in its telemetry.** We read that now instead of using the USB tap.
- **Your rev lights and the wheel's screen work in RaceRoom.** Using them normally interrupts the game's force. With the plugin carrying the force, there is nothing to interrupt.
- **An auto strength button, like iRacing's.** Drive a couple of laps, press it, and that car keeps the number. Bindable to a wheel button.
- **Engine data for every car in the game.** A shipped table now covers all 356 cars currently in the game with their real cylinder count and crank layout, so a V8 pulses like a V8. Without you needing to specify.

### Automatic LED pattern matching comes to Assetto Corsa

Our lighting enhancements had not reached Assetto Corsa until now. Usually the
game drives the wheel's lights itself. Now, the lights are fully controlled by
TF4ALL.

- **The lights fill where the car's own dash fills.** Cars in Assetto Corsa carry their own shift light data: the revs each light comes on at, the colors it uses, and how fast it flashes at the redline. The plugin reads that from the car itself, so your lights fill and flash in step with the dash in front of you rather than at a generic share of the rev range.
- **Automatically match your wheel's colors to the car you're driving.** Turn on "Match my wheel to the car I'm driving".
- **The wheel's screen no longer costs you the lights.** v0.3.0 ran the screen in Assetto Corsa for the first time, but the screen and the game were sharing one pipe to the wheel and the game's lights lost. With the lights ours, both run. Switching the screen on in Assetto Corsa switches our rev lights on with it.
- **If installed, the CSP Bridge now updates itself when SimHub starts.**

Needs the TF4ALL CSP Bridge.

### iRacing

- **Curb strikes, rumble strips and sharp hits are softened, and nothing else is.** iRacing reports each wheel's suspension speed six times a frame, beside the torque, and says when the car is on a strip. The torque samples from those instants are eased toward the cornering load before they reach the wheel, so a curb lands as a firm bump instead of a crack and a strip buzzes without hammering. Off the curb the force is exactly what the sim sent. New on the FFB tab, with a slider for how much to take out.

### Telemetry Based FFB

- **Stationary friction and the stationary spring come to Telemetry Based FFB, and the friction to Farming Simulator.** The wheel is firm when parked and eases off as you drive away, which is how most games feel natively. When the stationary spring is enabled the wheel pulls toward the center.

### Idle modes for the wheel's lights

- **Sweep.** A slow pass up the strip and back, at a speed you set.
- **Audio reactive.** The strip meters your computer's audio output, so the lights move with whatever you are listening to.

Both run when nobody is driving: no game, or the car sat still. Colors come
from whatever pattern you chose, and your revs take the lights back the moment
a game reports them. Off by default, under the rev lights.

### Defaults

- **A new install on a G923 starts at a master gain of 1.5.** Its belt drive renders the Trueforce stream fainter than the direct-drive wheels, so it begins with more headroom instead of asking you to find it. New installs only: if you already have the plugin set up, your gain is left exactly where you put it. The G PRO and RS50 start at 1.0.
- **The DirectInput engine's effects are retuned to match the wheel's own.** Damper, spring, friction, inertia and the waveforms were re-matched on the effect test bench against the wheel's native rendering after the engine changes in this release. A value you never touched moves to the new number; anything you tuned on the bench stays.
- **The iRacing preset ships with 3 ms of smoothing.** It rounds off the edges of the takeover's 360 Hz detail without adding lag you can feel.
- **The Forza Horizon preset ships with 6 ms of smoothing.** It removes the grain when turning the wheel at a standstill.

### Bug fixes

- **The wheel no longer pulls to one side when you pause, and effects no longer get stuck on.** SimHub keeps repeating the last telemetry it had, so the plugin held the force from that instant and pulled the wheel over, while sustained effects like the engine hum carried on playing. It now recognizes a pause, either because the game says so or because the readings stop changing, in every game fed through SimHub. First reported in RaceRoom.
- **"Hand the wheel back to the game while paused" is now off, for everyone once.** With pauses handled properly the plugin can stay on the wheel through one, so the lights and screen stay alive and there is no hand-over and hand-back each time. Tick it again if you prefer the game's own force feedback while paused.
- **The pause hand-back no longer flaps in Forza's menus.** Forza's pause menu alternates live and paused frames every few seconds, and the hand-back followed them, taking the wheel from the game's menu spring mid-swing each time. It now waits for the driver to move, throttle or rev before handing the wheel back.
- **Soft lock now works in Assetto Corsa, iRacing and RaceRoom.** In Assetto Corsa with the CSP Bridge, CSP applies its custom soft lock after the bridge has already handed the force over to the plugin, so the lock never reached the wheel. The bridge now renders it itself, using your settings from CSP's FFB Tweaks page (issue #43). In iRacing and RaceRoom the plugin now builds the stop from the sim's own steering position and lock, in the same shape. On by default for the takeovers, with a strength control beside the takeover settings on the FFB tab.
- **Removing the CSP Bridge puts your previous post-processing script back.** It used to leave CSP's slot pointing at the deleted script.
- **Fixed a regression introduced in v0.3.0 which caused the Telemetry Based FFB settings to remain hidden when the toggle was enabled.** They would not show until the plugin page was reloaded.
- **The peak limiter no longer lets a long slide along a wall hammer the wheel.** It capped how hard a hit landed but not how fast, so each reversal against a scraping wall arrived in a single millisecond. Its running average also learned from the hit, letting each reversal land higher the longer the slide went on. A hit above the limiter's minimum reference is now slowed as well, and the average no longer rises during a hit. Found in iRacing.
- **The force fades back in after a pause instead of stepping, in iRacing and under Telemetry Based FFB.** Coming back from a pause could land the full cornering force in one go.
- **The iRacing setup notice now appears when SimHub is started with iRacing already running.** It fired before SimHub had a window to sit on and died quietly. The same applies to the new RaceRoom notice.
- **iRacing could lose all force feedback until SimHub was restarted.** After a stand-down, a mode change or a switch to another game and back, the plugin's direct telemetry reader came back with an empty channel table, so the takeover had nothing to work from and the wheel went dead.
- **Inertia effects no longer buzz on the G923.** The wheel-motion estimate behind them had one filter stage too few, so above a few hertz it reported velocity, twenty times over, as acceleration, and the inertia effect turned into a violent damper. A second stage cures it at any strength.
- **Changing your light pattern mid-session no longer makes the lights flicker.** The wheel takes one writer at a time, and a pattern change arriving while something else was driving the lights produced a visible stutter. Only one thing writes to them at a time now.
- **No more unplugging your wheel to get force feedback working.** For some users the plugin's FFB capture would not work at all until they unplugged the wheel and plugged it back in. The plugin now detects a wheel that is not responding and tries to recover it on its own, with no replug needed.
- **A PlayStation G923 no longer needs G HUB run after every computer start.** That wheel comes back in PlayStation mode whenever it loses power, and in that mode the plugin cannot see it at all, so the only cure was to launch G HUB, wait for it to find the wheel, and close it again, every single time. The plugin now does the switch itself. It only ever does this to a wheel in that state, never to one that is already working.

### Interface

- **Spike reduction has been re-worded and is easier to understand.** Its two methods are named on the screen, Rate limiter and Peak limiter, and their numbers read as a percentage of full force instead of a raw count. The peak limiter also has its own default tuning now, rather than borrowing the rate limiter's number, and each preset keeps its own threshold.
- **Every number beside a slider can be typed into.** Most already could. The ones that could not have been brought into line, so an exact filter corner, redline or sweep time no longer has to be found by dragging.
- **Soft lock, curb strike softening and stationary friction each fold into their own section on the FFB tab.** The on/off switch sits in the section header, as it now does for the stationary spring too, so the row shows whether the feature is on without opening it. Strength, Damping and Smoothing sit together above them.
- **Reset FFB tuning puts spike reduction back too.** One button restores the force tuning for the game you are in, spike reduction included, and leaves your other games alone.
- **Simplified and shortened the LED pattern preview.**
- **Ten new guides in Help:** RaceRoom setup, how the force handover works, the CSP Bridge, force and lights, backup and sync, car facts, the dash, the home tile, tuning effects, and the wheel screen.
- **The stationary spring section shows only in games which support it.**
- **The What's new window is wider, resizable, and easier to read.** Wrapped notes render as paragraphs instead of running together.
- **RaceRoom is called RaceRoom.** It showed as RRRE in places.
